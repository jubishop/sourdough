**Status: baked November 19; actual bake observations and crumb-photo discussion recorded. User will add the photo to GitHub separately.**

## Reference versus actual settings

The research project's standard formula remains 100% whole wheat: 500 g King Arthur Golden Whole Wheat Flour added to the dough, 400 g main-dough water, 100 g 100%-hydration levain and 12 g salt (550 g total flour, 450 g total water; nominal 81.8% hydration). **These are reference recipe values, not a separate confirmation that every ingredient or handling setting was used on November 19.**

The user directly confirmed the following **actual oven schedule and measurements** (dial settings):

| Stage | Oven setting | Time | Result |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Covered | 465°F | 20 min | Very little/no apparent oven spring by lid removal |
| Uncovered, first phase | 315°F | 45 min | Center temperature measured at **205°F** after removal |
| Uncovered, additional phase | 315°F | **15 min more** | User confirmed the loaf reached the desired **208°F** target, but did not give a precise final thermometer reading; may have needed less than all 15 minutes |
| **Total** | | **80 min: 20 covered + 60 uncovered** | Target temperature achieved |

The first 65 minutes (20/45) proved **insufficient for the user's chosen 208°F endpoint** on this loaf. The extra 15 uncovered minutes were applied after removing and probing the loaf at 205°F. Repeated removal/probing was inconvenient and lost oven heat; the user would rather make one end-of-bake measurement.

Other actuals—mixing/autolyse, fold count, bulk endpoint and temperature, cold-proof time, and starter/levain feed quantities—were **not separately confirmed for this loaf**. Do not silently infer them from the published recipe.

## Fermentation observations

- The user reports the **mother starter was unusually sluggish**, apparently coincident with changing to King Arthur Golden Wheat flour; the cause is suspected, not established.
- **The levain and the dough's bulk fermentation were also unusually slow.** This was not just a sluggish maintenance jar.
- The user is trying to rebuild the starter's strength by **refeeding approximately every 12 hours**.
- Slow fermentation may influence gas production, proof state, crumb and overall spring, but does **not establish that the spring would occur later in the oven**. The user has not concluded that the loaf was underproofed at loading.

## Oven-spring observations and interpretation

- At **20 minutes covered**, the user saw **no meaningful visible expansion**.
- Unlike the November 17 interpretation that score opening *might* be mistaken for spring, the user deliberately watched for that issue this time and reports that the **whole loaf genuinely expanded after lid removal**. The exact timing and total size of this late expansion were **not measured**; it could have taken substantially longer than 20 minutes to finish.
- This is a useful **observation**, not proof that an extra covered stage would improve spring. Slow levain/bulk activity may confound any covered-time inference, and the explanation for the delayed-looking expansion remains uncertain.
- Covered sourdough baking is commonly started with about 20 minutes to retain steam while much of the spring occurs, but it is not a universal maximum or proof that this loaf must have finished springing by then.
- A return to **five covered minutes at 390°F after the 20 minutes at 465°F** was discussed as a possible *future* test. It has **not been selected for the next bake**. If a normally vigorous dough already finishes springing by 20 minutes, additional covered time purely for spring would have little rationale; if genuine spring continues well after lid removal, a controlled 25-minute total covered test could be informative.
