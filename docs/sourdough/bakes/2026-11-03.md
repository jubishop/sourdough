# November 3, 2026 — 100% whole-wheat sourdough bake

**Record type:** reconstructed from the November 3 discussion, three original photos, and the recipe site snapshot contemporaneous with the bake. The live site is updated before each new bake; this is a historical snapshot, not an instruction to overwrite future site changes.

## Actual reported outcome
- User's first assessment: **“It turned out pretty good I think.”**
- User subsequently said: **“The gumminess is basically gone.”** This is the strongest reported improvement relative to previous gummy loaves.
- The **bottom was still a bit too brown** (user's direct observation); photos in this archive show exterior and crumb, not the bottom of this November 3 loaf.
- The user said the loaf **“still blew out a lot”** and there was **“still a belly button.”** Those two remaining shaping/expansion concerns should not be misreported as solved.
- Exterior appearance was golden-brown with good rise and pronounced spreading at the X scoring.
- Crumb appears fairly well aerated for 100% whole wheat, with broadly distributed small and medium pores and some larger voids; no pronounced continuous dense/gummy base band. There is a small bottom-center pocket and converging/compressed fold consistent with residual underside seam gathering.
- Photo appearance does not establish an exact dough temperature or definitive proofing level.

## Contemporaneous recipe/process snapshot
- Flour: **500 g 100% whole-wheat flour**.
- Added water: **400 g**, plus **100 g 100%-hydration levain** and **12 g salt**.
- Totals: **550 g flour, 450 g water**, nominal **81.8% hydration** (excluding any extra water transferred from wet hands).
- **90-minute autolyse**.
- Up to **six squash-and-fold rounds**, at least **20 minutes apart**.
- Bulk rise in a **74–78°F proof box**, ending when the dough meets the **1.0 L** mark at the glass edge of the vessel. This is a vessel reading rather than a precisely proven percentage volume rise.
- **15-minute** pre-shaping chill while still in the bulk container (previous method had involved roughly an hour); the chill's practical purpose is easier container release without damage.
- Shaping followed by **8–16 hours cold proof**. Actual elapsed cold-proof time for this individual bake was not separately recorded.
- **Large X scoring**.
- Preheat Dutch oven **at 475°F for at least 60 minutes** with **two King Arthur silicone bread liners on the bottom**, present through preheating and baking according to the contemporaneous website snapshot.
- Covered: **10 minutes at 465°F**, followed by **20 minutes at 390°F**.
- Uncovered: **45 minutes at 315°F**.
- Brush with olive oil immediately after baking; cool for **at least 3 hours** before slicing.
- The uncovered time of **45 minutes** was an explicit update in the recipe used for this bake.

## Analysis, with uncertainty kept visible
- A longer, gentler uncovered phase **may** have contributed to the marked reduction in perceived gumminess, but a single bake with other process differences cannot establish that it was the sole cause.
- Strong oven spring plus distributed crumb pores do not point to severe over- or underproofing. Avoid changing fermentation solely from the photos.
- The small residual cavity/fold and continuing blowout warrant a cleaner shaping test; the original October 31 belly-button problem was not fully eliminated.
- The darker-than-desired bottom is a separate crust/heat-management issue. Do not conflate it with crumb gumminess, which is nearly solved by user report.

## User's next experiment / exact ambiguity
- User said they plan to **“add a second perforated silicone liners”** because the bottom is still too brown.
- **IMPORTANT discrepancy, intentionally unresolved:** The contemporaneous recipe snapshot already mentions **two King Arthur silicone liners during preheat and baking**; the user’s “second perforated” wording may refer to a different perforated-liner type or a different effective count. **Do not silently assume this means a third liner or that the website’s two liners were not used.** Confirm precise current/next liner setup when it matters.
- User also intends to **shape a bit less aggressively** in view of residual blowout and belly button. Practical guidance discussed: use only one or two gentle, short tensioning pulls as needed; avoid creating a tight underside knot.
- Retain the achieved good fermentation/gumminess results while assessing the effects of bottom-liner and shaping adjustments; avoid attributing a later outcome to one variable if both were changed.
