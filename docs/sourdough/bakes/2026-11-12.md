# 2026-11-12 — 15-minute autolyse result; 1-hour autolyse plan

**Date caveat:** November 12 is when the user shared and assessed the finished crumb photo; the precise mixing and baking dates/times were not confirmed. The next experiment below is a **plan**, not a completed bake.

## Observed — shorter-autolyse loaf

- **Autolyse:** **15 minutes**, intentionally reduced from the familiar **90-minute** flour-and-water rest.
- **Bulk endpoint:** **1 US quart (~946 mL), measured at the glass edge** of the proofing vessel. The user has repeatedly baked at **both 1 quart and 1.0 L**, so these are well-known settings rather than an unfamiliar fermentation trial.
- **Finished texture:** **No improvement in perceived gumminess**. User clarified the unpleasant crumb feel is predominantly **rubbery and chewy**, not sticky/paste-like.
- **Dough handling:** Dough **felt essentially the same at shaping time** compared with longer-autolyse loaves.
- **Crumb photograph:** [Crumb cross-section](../photos/2026-11-12-crumb.jpeg) (original `IMG_1050.jpeg`, 2000×1500). There is an enormous, irregular cavity/structural discontinuity toward the upper left, but **the crumb toward the outer edges, away from the shaping defect, actually looks fairly good to the user**. Do **not** describe the entire crumb as poor or uniformly underfermented simply because the cavity is dramatic.
- **Recurring shaping problem:** A deep underside **“belly button”** formed again. The user believes they tend to shape too aggressively and suspects that shaping, rather than shortened autolyse, caused the conspicuous void/fold. This is plausible but not proven.
- **Other parameters:** Established working ingredients are 500 g 100%-whole-wheat flour, 400 g water, 100 g 100%-hydration levain and 12 g salt (**550 g total flour / 450 g total water, ~81.8% nominal hydration**). No independent verification of all times/temperatures and individual steps of this particular loaf was supplied; do not reconstruct them as observed facts.

### Interpretation, not established mechanism

- The 15-minute autolyse **did not reduce rubbery/chewy mouthfeel**, weakening the specific idea that cutting the usual 90-minute autolyse would solve it.
- The large local cavity may be a **shaping/seam/air-entrapment artifact**, while the peripheral crumb looks relatively sound. Do not conflate that localized defect with the **independent texture concern**.
- Excessive amylase remains only a hypothesis; **rubbery** is not synonymous with sticky, paste-like starch degradation. Long uncovered baking has been tried extensively without reliably removing the unwanted texture.
- No clear handling disadvantage to 15 minutes was reported, though the user is **not** adopting the short autolyse as the standard.

## Next-bake plan — revised November 12; not yet baked

**Superseded later on November 12:** the next bake became a flour trial, King Arthur Golden Wheat in place of Bob's Red Mill, keeping the 1-hour autolyse below. See the [November 13 planned trial](2026-11-13.md).

**Latest user decision (supersedes both earlier plans that day):** Use a **1-hour autolyse**, 30 minutes shorter than the previous **about 90 minutes**. The reason was not stated. The user first chose a 2.5–3-hour autolyse, then zero autolyse, then replaced both with 1 hour; neither earlier plan was baked. The user then judged 1 hour too small a change to be an experiment and made it the **Baseline** as well, so both site recipes match.

- **Procedure:** Mix **500 g whole-wheat flour + 400 g water** until no dry pockets remain, cover, and rest for **1 hour**. Then add **100 g ripe 100%-hydration levain + 12 g salt** and continue with the usual folds and bulk. Every other step follows the site's Baseline.
- **Bulk target:** **1.0 L at the glass edge**. User prefers this slightly over **1 US quart (~946 mL)** and has repeatedly baked at both; the ~5.7% difference is small and familiar enough, in their judgment, to evaluate the primary autolyse distinction. Note the endpoint change transparently.
- **Hydration:** Retain the standard **81.8% nominal** (550 g flour total, 450 g water total). Lower hydration makes this dough too hard to handle. Retain usual levain, salt, fold schedule, cold proof, insulation/liners, bake profile, and cooling when practicable.
- **Shaping:** Try **gentler/minimal shaping** without forceful countertop tension-building pulls, because the repeated underside belly button may cause large isolated cavities. This is a parallel handling intention, not evidence that shaping causes the rubbery mouthfeel.
- **Assess separately:** (1) dough feel after the 1-hour rest, (2) dough feel during folds and at shaping, (3) **rubbery/chewy** mouthfeel after comparable cooling, (4) crumb **away from shaping defects** for evenness/open pockets, and (5) spring, spread, and belly-button cavity. Compare with earlier 90-minute loaves.

### Other future alternatives — not selected next

The longer **150–180-minute autolyse**, **zero autolyse**, a different whole-wheat flour, fermentation adjustments, a **15–20% flour scald** (flour treated with boiling water), and hotter initial covered baking remain candidate tests. Scalding could improve softness or make a more gel-like texture; it is not a proven fix for rubbery crumb. Extending uncovered baking further is low priority after previous unsuccessful attempts.
