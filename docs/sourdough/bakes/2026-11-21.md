# 2026-11-21 — partial covered-phase expansion, tight slightly gummy crumb, and fermentation priority

**Status: baked November 21.** Notes are based on the baker's direct observations and the three shared exterior/crumb photographs. The photographs have not yet been added to this repository.

## Actual observations and recipe reference

- **Bulk endpoint:** The baker says this loaf went **closer to 1.0 L at the glass edge**, rather than the older 1 US quart (~946 mL) mark. This is an approximate reported endpoint, not a precisely measured dough volume. The current published recipe targets 1.0 L.
- **Baking schedule discussed for this loaf:** 20 minutes covered at 465°F, then 10 minutes still covered at 390°F, then 45 minutes uncovered at 315°F (**75 minutes total**). This was the prior confirmed next-bake plan and the schedule consistently used in the November 21 discussion; there was no separate measurement of oven temperature.
- **Center temperature:** **Not measured** on November 21. The baker strongly suspects it reached at least 208°F given the baking schedule, but this is an inference, **not a measured result**. Do not log 208°F as achieved.
- **Cooling:** The baker **always allows loaves to cool fully before slicing**, including this one. Premature slicing is not a plausible explanation of this loaf's reported gumminess.
- **Ingredients/process:** The current site reference is King Arthur Golden Wheat 100% whole-wheat flour, 550 g total flour / 450 g total water (nominal 81.8% hydration), 100 g 100%-hydration levain, and 12 g salt, with paddle mixing and no autolyse. The baker did not separately reconfirm every ingredient, fold, dough temperature, proof-box setting, bulk duration, shaping detail, or cold-proof duration for this particular loaf. Do not present those reference settings as independent actual measurements.

## Exterior and oven spring

- The finished loaf was **not much better overall** than recent disappointing loaves. It spread broadly rather than displaying the desired strong vertical rise.
- The X-shaped scoring opened widely, exposing rough interior surfaces; the crust looks well browned in the photos.
- **New observation compared with November 19:** At lid removal after the **20 + 10 minute covered stages**, the baker could **already see some oven spring**. On November 19, the baker reported conspicuous expansion after removing the lid at minute 20. This contrast is useful, but the amount and timing of *actual whole-loaf height increase* versus score opening were not measured. In particular, it is unknown whether the visible expansion on November 21 happened during the first 20 minutes or the extra 10 minutes covered at 390°F.
- Do **not** conclude that the extra covered time caused improvement or that poor fermentation necessarily delayed oven spring on previous bakes.

## Crumb and eating quality

- The cut face shows a **predominantly tight, fine and fairly even network of small holes**, with scattered larger irregular pockets, especially toward the upper portion and left edge.
- There is a **denser, finely packed band toward the bottom**, most apparent through the center/right; a few elongated, slit-like voids may reflect stretching or layering, but their cause cannot be identified from a photograph.
- After full cooling, the baker reports the crumb is **still a bit gummy**. The desired less-rubbery texture, larger and more uniform pockets, and greater vertical spring are **not yet achieved**.
- The baker's sensory report, not appearance alone, establishes the lingering gumminess.

## Interpretation and next investigation

**Current leading investigation: fermentation.** Starter, levain, and bulk were unusually sluggish on the November 19 bake following a flour change. The November 21 appearance, modest improvement in covered-phase expansion, fairly tight crumb, and residual gumminess keep fermentation state and gas retention high on the list. However, these photos **do not prove underfermentation**; dough strength, shaping, hydration, and enzymatic effects remain alternatives.

- **Next priority agreed November 21:** Investigate fermentation rather than extending oven time or immediately reducing hydration. Check starter/levain vigor and maturity; record actual dough temperature, elapsed bulk time from levain addition, visual gas/dome development, and the endpoint at the glass edge. A controlled comparison to a **slightly later bulk endpoint than ~1.0 L** may be worthwhile *after* starter vigor stabilizes, but **no new exact endpoint or change to the published recipe has been approved**.
- Keep the **20/10/45 schedule** and other variables steady while isolating fermentation if feasible. The user agrees fermentation is the next major area to check, not that a specific new bulk endpoint has already been selected.
- Prior much longer uncovered baking failed to eliminate gumminess; 208°F internal is a useful completion check, not a guarantee of an open or non-gummy crumb. The November 21 center temperature remains unknown.
- Continue comparing crumb after complete cooling, basal compression, overall loaf height versus cut opening, and sensory chewiness.
