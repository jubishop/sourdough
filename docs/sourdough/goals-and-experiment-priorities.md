# 100% Whole-Wheat Sourdough — Goals and Experiment Priorities

**First-pass ranking:** November 9, 2026. **Status:** Proposed experimental order; user can reorder at any time. Existing experiment IDs are unchanged. No published recipe or next-bake instruction was changed by creating this plan.

## User's goal hierarchy (most important first)

1. **Drier-feeling, less gummy finished crumb.** Reduce residual tackiness / damp or gummy mouthfeel after full cooling, while avoiding a harsh, hard crust. Extensive previous baking trials show that merely extending oven drying does *not* solve this issue; do **not** prioritise more oven drying as a remedy. This goal concerns the inside of the bread, not making the raw dough stiffer for its own sake.
2. **Lighter, airier crumb with larger, well-distributed air pockets.** Prefer a more open, even interior over isolated large voids caused by shaping defects; maintain a pleasantly tender crumb in a 100%-whole-wheat loaf.
3. **More vertical oven spring.** Increase height and rise rather than lateral spread; preserve good structure and avoid major side blowouts.

When objectives conflict, generally respect the above order, but note any meaningful sacrifice to the lower goals. The user is already close to acceptable gumminess in recent bakes and is seeking incremental gains, not a completely different loaf.

## Current working controls / active bake

- Flour: 500 g King Arthur stone-ground whole wheat, plus 50 g flour carried in 100 g of 100%-hydration whole-wheat levain; total 550 g flour.
- Water: 400 g in autolyse plus 50 g in levain; total 450 g, **81.8% hydration**. Salt: **12 g**. Autolyse: **90 minutes**.
- **Proofing box: fixed 72°F. Bulk endpoint: 1.0 L measured at the glass edge.** These supersede the previous 76°F / 1 US quart (\~946 mL) setting. User moved the line to 1.0 L because the dough dome is flatter at 72°F. This is a practical current working calibration, **not proof** that both marks correspond to identical fermentation.
- **In-progress experiment #6:** two fold sessions at T+30 and T+60 minutes after incorporating levain and salt, instead of four sessions roughly 20 minutes apart. Folds occur at kitchen temperature, then dough goes into the 72°F proofing box until the 1.0 L mark. Do not describe the outcome as known until this loaf is assessed. Because temperature and the marked endpoint also changed relative to last bake, treat a striking result as suggestive, not perfectly causal.
- Maintain the accepted **no pre-shaping chill** and previously successful bottom insulation and bake profile; in particular, **do not extend the uncovered bake to chase gumminess**.
- The source recipe website may differ; this note records the user's conversational working settings, not verified deployed code.

## How to interpret the next few bakes

- Prefer one deliberate change at a time once the working 72°F / 1.0 L settings settle; do not make a change merely because a previous loaf was slightly different.
- **First evaluate the current two-fold loaf** for height (prefer actual height and footprint), spread, score opening, and bottom-of-crumb compression, as well as crumb moisture and pocket distribution after comparable full cooling.
- If two folds yield a weaker, flatter loaf, do not assume more extensibility is desirable: revisit the strength/extensibility balance before continuing down the list.
- If the first priority (less gumminess) is already effectively met, feel free to reweight the next experiments toward openness and vertical rise. Whole-wheat bran limits the attainable openness compared with refined white flour; avoid judging the loaf solely by large isolated holes.
- **Enzyme hypothesis:** possible starch/protein breakdown is one explanation for tackiness, not a confirmed diagnosis. A reduction in autolyse time is worth testing, but much enzymatic activity also happens later, and measured dough hydration, flour characteristics, fermentation level and baked starch setting matter.
- Do not infer that more drying in the oven should fix sticky crumb; prior direct trials have argued against that approach.

## Future trials (unprioritized)

- **Hotter covered start (variant of experiment #3).** Try 475–500°F (245–260°C) at the start of the covered bake. Keep the rest of the bake profile, including covered and uncovered times, fixed. The suggested hypothesis is that the crumb heats through the temperature range where amylase is active more quickly. Compare fully cooled crumb gumminess, crumb openness, oven spring, and bottom-crust color.
- **Scald a portion of the whole-wheat flour.** Consider if residual gumminess remains after testing a shorter autolyse and accounting for the results of the longer-bake trials. Scald 15–20% of the total whole-wheat flour (about 83–110 g of 550 g, taken from the main mix) with boiling water before mixing. Let the scald cool before adding it. Scalding gelatinizes some of the starch and changes how water is held in the dough. Deduct scald water from the main mix water to keep total hydration constant, including the water and flour in the levain. Compare crumb gumminess, crumb openness, and vertical oven spring after the same full cooling time.
