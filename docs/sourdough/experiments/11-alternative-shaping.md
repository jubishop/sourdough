# Sourdough Future Experiment #11 — Alternative Shaping Techniques

**Added:** November 6, 2026
**Status:** Unprioritized future experiment family; **each method is a separate bake**, not a combination.
**Question:** Can we create enough structure for a well-risen oval 100% whole-wheat loaf without gathering a deep underside belly-button cavity?

## Why this matters
- The 100%-whole-wheat dough already feels quite firm after the usual bulk and fold rounds.
- Prior countertop tightening pulls created an underside knot. On November 6, the dough produced an especially large knot **even though no countertop pulls were used**—it was shaped only by cupping with the hands.
- The pre-shape fridge has now been eliminated from the *baseline standard*, because dough releases from its container well without it.
- **Bulk target remains undecided:** the live website reportedly has separate Baseline and Experiment recipes, and the *Experiment* ends bulk at **1 US quart (~946 mL)** rather than the previous **1.0 L**. Keep fermentation fixed while comparing shaping trials; never treat the 1-qt endpoint as proven by itself.

## Individual shaping-method trials

### A. Broad envelope folds; no counter dragging
Previously discussed November 5. Turn out dough gently and spread only as needed into a thick rounded rectangle/square. Fold the left, right, top, and bottom portions across as **broad, flat overlapping layers**, not a tight central pinch or little package. Transfer seam-up into the floured oval banneton. **Do not flip the dough and drag it around the countertop.** Compare underside fold and loaf spring.

### B. Gentle fold-and-roll batard
Turn out into a thick rectangle, fold sides inward with minimal degassing, then roll into an elongated oval log suitable for the user's oval banneton, creating modest surface tension through the roll rather than by cupping/dragging the entire ball. Seal only the longitudinal seam **lightly**, not by pushing all the dough into a center point. Transfer seam-up into banneton. Compare seam geometry, shape, and oven spring.

### C. Minimal counter tightening, only if worth testing later
The historically discussed comparison: use at most **two short, very gentle pulls** (versus the older roughly four stronger pulls), stopping when the outside is barely taut. Do not flip the dough halfway through the shaping process to inspect—this was previously rejected because it would disturb tension. Photograph underside as soon as it goes seam-up into the banneton. This is a trial, not a default recommendation; hand-cupping without pulling also failed to prevent the knot.

### D. No deliberate shaping (tracked separately as Experiment #10)
Move directly from bulk container to banneton with no preshape, pinching, folding, rolling, hand cupping, or dragging. Reference `/Sourdough/Experiment-10-No-Shaping.md`. This is the cleanest counterpoint to methods A–C.

## Controls and evaluation
- Continue **100% whole wheat**, consistent flour/hydration/levain/salt and usual bulk folds, **no pre-shaping refrigeration**, the same chosen bulk target, cold proof, score, and bake profile, including the two perforated silicone liners plus circular King Arthur silicone liner specified in the current recipes.
- Keep only one shaping method per loaf. Note degree of tension, handling time, seam appearance, and any collapse on turn-out.
- Photograph underside immediately after transfer, then baked top/side, then crumb after full cooling.
- Compare **belly-button size/inner fold**, bottom compression, crumb uniformity, spread, loaf height, oven spring, side tearing, and ease of handling.
- A technique giving no underside cavity but a noticeably flatter loaf is a tradeoff, not an automatic win.
- Hold judgments until bake outcomes are available and avoid prematurely migrating any shaping method to Baseline.
